# Blade Vale — The Real Endless World

**One line:** a single persistent online world that doesn't exist until a real person goes there. Claim a stretch of frontier no human has ever visited, and the world generates itself into being around you — terrain, roads, towns, factions, history — and stitches it permanently into everyone's map. The world only grows where people actually go. Nothing is pre-built; nothing is stored until someone visits it.

This is not a pitch deck. **It already runs.** ~36,000 lines of zero-asset JavaScript, backed by an always-on server that simulates a five-nation war on a procedurally generated hex world 24/7. Every soldier in it has a name and a persistent career. Terrain generation is deterministic and byte-identical on client and server — the trick that makes an *endless* world cheap.

---

## The three pillars

### 1. The endless world (server-authoritative procedural generation)
The world is generated by a **shared deterministic kernel** (`sim/terra.js`) that produces byte-identical output on client and server. Chunks generate on first visit and persist in SQLite. Storage grows with *explored* world, not world size. The server never streams geometry — it ships tiny seeds and the client regenerates locally, so bandwidth per player is minimal and offline play degrades gracefully.

The frontier already *coheres*: beyond the five heartland powers, every region condenses into a named, coloured realm with contiguous borders — infinite, deterministic, and byte-identical on client and server — instead of anonymous one-off towns. The politics don't thin out as you ride past the map's edge; they keep going.

**The leap:** extend determinism from geometry to the *whole stack* — terrain, rules, history, and how a region "works," all reproducible from a seed plus a versioned algorithm-set. And a social layer on top: **claim virgin frontier, name it, own it, and it becomes real for everyone.**

### 2. AI-authored generators (the generative layer that dissolves the cost problem)
We are **not** putting an LLM in the runtime content path. No per-visit inference bill, no latency, no "the world contradicts its own history" problem — because the world isn't authored by a model at play-time. It's generated by deterministic *algorithms*.

The generative layer is at **authoring time**. Today those algorithms are written by pairing a human with Claude — the entire procedural stack (terrain kernel, settlement generator, road network, warfare sim) is code produced that way. So the "AI cost" is a subscription and hours, and the runtime stays cheap and perfectly consistent forever.

**The leap:** close the loop. An LLM authors *new generators* from a spec; each candidate runs through a determinism + quality harness; the best ones promote into the library. Human-in-the-loop first, then a purpose-built model trained specifically to author generation-algorithms. "The server generates how the world works" means **AI writing versioned, deterministic, forever-consistent code** — not a model narrating at runtime.

### 3. Living NPC AI (the world moves whether or not you're online)
NPCs are real actors, not scenery. They **form and break alliances, found cities, build roads out to them, raise armies, and fight wars** — on their own, so the world is alive whether or not a player is logged in.

The scaffolding runs today: a five-nation war ticks 24/7, a diplomacy engine tracks faction relations, settlements generate from the landform, armies muster→march→siege. Garrisons now patrol the *whole realm they hold* — riding out to guard their villages and castles, and turning neutral columns back at the border without bloodshed. But the intelligence is **rule-based, not smart** — a simulation, not a mind.

**The leap:** NPCs as goal-driven agents that make *legible* decisions a player can read and react to — this warlord expanded because he was boxed in; these two nations allied because a third grew strong. Same philosophy as the world generator: deterministic, persistent, consistent behavior whose choices become permanent world history. A world that keeps building cities and fighting wars while everyone sleeps is the entire "living" promise.

## The one hard bet (what the CTO owns)

Today's architecture is a *shared deterministic simulation*: the client can regenerate the world locally, so the server ships seeds, not geometry. That's genius for bandwidth and offline resilience.

The end-vision — *server authoritatively authors how the world works, clients stay thin renderers* — pulls partly the other way. Reconciling the two — **determinism for the infinite terrain, an AI-authored generative layer for the meaning, agentic NPCs for the life, at a scale of millions** — is the core engineering of the company. That is the CTO seat.

The real job jar:
1. **Determinism discipline** — one float drift forks the world. The moat and the fragility.
2. **Scale** — one Node process + one SQLite file today → thousands → millions. The headline mandate.
3. **The generator-authoring loop** — the spec, the eval harness, the path to auto and to a purpose-built model.
4. **Living NPC agents** — rules today; agentic, legible, deterministic tomorrow.
5. **Server-authoritative trust** — friends-on-LAN today → strangers-online via deterministic replay verification, not server-side battle simulation.

## Scale posture (stated plainly)
- **Concurrent today:** dozens of testers. The ~1,300 entities per tick are NPCs, not players.
- **Near target:** 10k for the funded alpha, architected so a million doesn't mean repainting the foundation.
- **Why it's winnable:** seed-based generation means no geometry streaming; generation is embarrassingly parallel by chunk key; each world is structurally one SQLite file, so world/region sharding is a structural move, not a rewrite.
- **Cross-engine determinism is an afterthought, on purpose.** We're deterministic on whatever engines have the players (V8/Blink first); we harden for another engine only when its market share forces it. Support follows popularity, not ideology.

## What must exist in 2 months
The vertical slice that proves the whole thesis, running on the internet for a small invite cohort: **a player claims virgin frontier no one has visited, the generator loop authors a living region around them — terrain, roads, a town, a faction with a stake — and it persists for everyone.** That demo *is* the pitch for what comes next.

## Business model
Free web funnel → Steam (~$4.99) → mobile. The paid pillar is the always-on living world and persistent characters — the thing you can't get from a copy of the client. Hard "no" on pay-to-win, loot boxes, and energy timers (see `ROADMAP.md` → *We Will NOT Do*).
